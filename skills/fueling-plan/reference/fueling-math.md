# Fueling math

The numbers the skill uses, and where they come from. General sports-nutrition
guidance for trained endurance athletes — not medical advice, and not tuned to
any individual's gut tolerance until Bryce logs real sessions against it.

## Carbohydrate rate by session duration

Applied per session, using planned duration from TrainingPeaks.

| Planned duration | Target carb rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| < 60 min | 0 g/hr | Nothing needed. Muscle glycogen covers it. |
| 60–90 min | ~30 g/hr | Optional below 75 min unless intensity is high. |
| 90–150 min | ~60 g/hr | The reliable ceiling for glucose-only products. |
| 150–240 min | 60–90 g/hr | Needs a glucose:fructose blend to exceed 60. |
| > 240 min | 80–100 g/hr | Upper end only with gut training. |

Intensity nudges within the band, it does not jump bands. If planned IF ≥ 0.80
or the session is flagged as a key/race-pace effort, take the top of the range.
Recovery and easy aerobic work sits at the bottom.

## The 60 g/hr ceiling — why it binds here

Above roughly 60 g/hr, a single carbohydrate source saturates the SGLT1
intestinal transporter. Getting past that requires fructose alongside glucose,
which uses a separate transporter (GLUT5). Combined, absorption reaches
90–120 g/hr; the current preferred ratio at high intakes is about 1:0.8
glucose:fructose.

**This constrains Bryce's current pantry directly.** G.1.M Sport+ is
Cluster Dextrin, a glucose polymer with no fructose listed. The Go Gels'
carb sources are unverified. So until a product's label confirms a
glucose:fructose blend, the skill caps planned intake at **60 g/hr** even when
duration would justify 90.

Do not quietly plan 90 g/hr out of glucose-only products. That is the recipe
for GI distress, and it is the most likely way this skill would hurt rather
than help. Say the ceiling is binding, and say what would lift it.

## Sport adjustment

- **Bike** — tolerates the full range. Drink mix, gels, and solid food all fine.
- **Run** — cap at ~60 g/hr regardless of duration. Gut jostling raises GI risk.
  Prefer gels and fluid; no bars mid-run.
- **Brick / same-day double** — fuel each session on its own terms, then check
  the combined daily caffeine total across both.

## Sodium

Baseline **500–800 mg per hour** of sweating. Push toward and past the top in
heat, or on any session over three hours.

Raw Replenish is ~1000 mg sodium per serving — built to be mixed roughly one
serving per litre. It carries no meaningful carbohydrate, so it is a companion
to a carb source, never a replacement for one.

Go Gels contribute 110 mg each, and G.1.M Sport+ 350 mg per scoop. Count those
before adding Replenish, or the day runs salty.

## Fluid

- Moderate conditions: 500–750 mL/hr
- Heat, or anything over 3 hr: 750–1000 mL/hr

Chicago summer humidity puts most of Bryce's July–August long sessions at the
top of that range.

## Caffeine budget — the real constraint

Performance dosing is roughly 3–6 mg/kg body mass, taken 45–60 min before the
effort that matters. Practical daily ceiling is ~400 mg for most adults.

The pantry makes this easy to blow through without noticing:

| Source | Caffeine |
|---|---|
| G.1.M Sport+ | **150 mg per scoop** |
| Go Gel CAF (Mixed Berry, Watermelon) | 75 mg each |
| BUM Energy Pouch | unverified |
| PUMP / Thuper Pump / Essential Charged | pre-workout, varies |

Two scoops of Sport+ is 300 mg on its own. Add two CAF gels and it is 450 mg —
over the ceiling, before counting coffee or a pre-workout.

Rules the skill enforces:

1. Total the day's caffeine across every session and every product.
2. Warn above ~400 mg. Rebalance toward caffeine-free options rather than
   just flagging it.
3. Front-load caffeine into the session where it matters. Do not spread it.
4. Nothing caffeinated within ~6 hr of bedtime — worth stating explicitly on
   evening sessions, given the Peak Sleep in the cupboard.

## Gut training

Reaching 90+ g/hr takes 4–6 weeks of progressive intake in one or two sessions
per week. If Bryce wants to move past 60 g/hr, that is the path — and it needs
a product with fructose in it first.
